### A1. The target cup

What the photo shows: a **machined copper cup** held open-end-first between gloved fingers. Inside, a polished
hemispherical (bowl) cavity of roughly Ø 20–25 mm; at its apex a small matte-grey **Li disc of about Ø 6–8 mm**; at the
open end a **thick rim** (a few mm) and a cylindrical outside. This is the CN neutron-line target: arXiv:2605.12005 §4.1
(`refs/CN_quasimono_neutrons_arXiv2605.12005.txt` lines 158–195) describes "a metallic lithium layer pressed into a
**300 µm-thick copper backing** that also serves as the beam stopper … pressed onto the copper backing using paired
moulds", air-cooled, 10 W. (Sizes read off the photo against a glove fingertip, ±30 %; **ask for the drawing**.)

| §17 assumed | the photo / the CN paper | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| hemisphere, concave to the beam, Li on the inside at the apex, beam through the open end, cup = end of the vacuum | the same | the §17 reading of idea 1 is right |
| wall 200 µm (his message) | the existing cup is **300 µm** (CN paper) | slightly worse than §17's 13.8° / 0.40 d: a 300 µm flat Cu is already 0.37 d (§15), and the dome's tangent path grows as √t. Trace rows added (A3) |
| inner radius R = 17.5 mm (DN40) | R ≈ 10–12 mm | the trace is insensitive (R 10: 14.7°, R 25: 13.5°) |
| a 12.7 mm SS CF flange at the equator; the "flange moved back" variant had nothing at the equator | a **thick Cu rim at the equator**, part of the cup | the rim sits where the flange was modelled, so the headline number holds; the "flange moved back" row (12.8°) was optimistic. `dome(rim=(w, h))` added |
| Li evaporated on the inside | a **pressed** disc (paired moulds, Ar glovebox), Ø 6–8 mm | fine for the physics (pressed Li 10–40 µm is their routine). But see the spot size below |
| "small material around 90°" (his pro) | legs at 90° from the apex run along the tangent plane and cross √(2Rt) ≈ 2.1–2.6 mm of Cu (15–18 % X₀) before leaving the shell | §17's verdict stands: a Cu cup costs ×1.8 in days; make the dome Be or Al with a Mo liner, or use the window |

**Beam spot against the Li disc (new).** The study's beam is σ = 2 mm (CN: 2–8 mm FWHM). The fraction of a Gaussian
beam outside the disc:

| σ | outside r = 3 mm | r = 4 mm | r = 6 mm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 mm | 1 % | 0 | 0 |
| **2 mm** | **32 %** | **13 %** | 1 % |
| 3.4 mm | 68 % | 50 % | 21 % |

Protons that miss the Li hit bare Cu: no signal, no γ above 1 MeV, only heat and hydrogen, and the current-to-yield
normalisation is off (the 478 keV PIGE line, not the current, then measures the Li). Ask for a **Li disc of Ø ≥ 12 mm**,
or a spot of σ ≤ 1 mm (the thermal limits at σ = 1 mm are in TARGET_OPTIONS §3 and are fine at LNL's currents).

### A2. The beam line

What the photo shows: a horizontal **DN40 stainless line** on floor stands, axis ~1.2 m above a tiled lab floor (a
ladder and benches behind: a lab or the end of the CN 0° line). From left to right within ~40 cm of the end: a KF joint,
a tee down with a bellows to a pump, a **VAT gate valve** with its pneumatic actuator pointing up, a cross with a
vertical stub, and an **end flange** (it looks larger than DN40, perhaps DN63; the cup or window would mount there).

Matches §17: a DN40 tube (1.65 mm SS wall, R 17.5) ending in a CF flange, air on the outside. ✓

Not in §17, and it matters for the arms:
- **Mechanical clash.** The Micromegas span ±180 mm along the beam at 204 mm from the axis, with the SiPM wall and
  plastics behind to ~0.5 m and their frames. The valve actuator (≈ 15–20 cm above the axis) and the vertical stub are
  inside the top arm's footprint if the target is within ~25 cm of them. Needed: **≥ 30 cm of plain pipe (Ø ≤ 70 mm with
  its flanges) upstream of the target**, the valve actuator turned down or sideways, no stands under the last 30 cm.
- **Material in view.** Leptons at 90–131° cross the pipe only within ~40 mm of the target (§17) and see any hardware
  with radius > 1.1·|y|; the valve body (±~70 mm) is out of view only ≥ 7 cm back. With ≥ 30 cm it is harmless.
- **Height.** The bottom arm needs ~60 cm below the axis: 1.2 m is enough.
- **The end flange.** If it is DN63 (17.5 mm thick, r to 57 mm) there is more steel at the equator than modelled; the
  trace result does not change (legs past ~120° already cross it) but Geant4 round 5 should use the real flange.
- **Forced air.** The fan, nozzle or duct must stay out of the 45–135° cone: cool from 0° (downstream) only.
- **Current readout.** The cup is the Faraday cup; it needs an insulating break from the pipe and a biased suppressor
  ring upstream for the secondary electrons (TARGET_OPTIONS §14 says this for the ring design; it applies here too).

## B. Audit findings, ranked

### B1. The diamond-window pressure stress is low by ×2.7–3.3 (error; changes a recommendation on the live page)

`opt_shapes.membrane()` used the Hencky **membrane** solution, 0.423 (E p² a²/t²)^(1/3), which has no bending stiffness
and so no bending stress at a **clamped** edge. A clamped plate under pressure has a boundary layer at the edge where the
bending stress is added to the membrane stress. With Roark's large-deflection clamped-plate solution ("fixed and held",
Table 11.11 case 3; its small-deflection limit is the classical 0.75 q a²/t² and y = q a⁴/(64 D), and its large-deflection
limit scales like the membrane), E = 1050 GPa, ν = 0.07, 1 bar (`plate_stress()`, `shapes_pressure.csv`):

| diamond | Ø35 mm (r 17.5): edge MPa | centre | membrane-only (old) | Ø25 (r 12.5): edge | Ø20 (r 10): edge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 µm | **1260** | 700 | 460 | 1110 | 1020 |
| 100 µm | **970** | 540 | 290 | 830 | 700 |
| 200 µm | **600** | 320 | 180 | 320 | **200** |
| 300 µm | **280** | 150 | 140 | 135 | 85 |
| 500 µm | 94 | 50 | 100 | 48 | 30 |

Polycrystalline CVD diamond breaks at ~0.5–1.2 GPa in bending (the nucleation side is the weak one, and the tension side
at a clamped edge is the pressurised, air-side face). So:
- **The §17 / design-page advice "100–200 µm over the full Ø35 mm (290–180 MPa)" is not safe: the edge stress is
  0.6–1.0 GPa.** The 45° tilt makes the window a 35 × 49 mm ellipse, which raises the stress by another ×1.3–1.5.
- Safe options: **≥ 400–500 µm at Ø35 mm** (tilted), or **200–300 µm over a Ø20–25 mm aperture**. The lepton cost of
  thicker diamond is small: §17 has +0.2° / +0.55° for 100 / 200 µm; a Highland extrapolation (45° incidence, half the
  legs) gives ≈ +0.8° for 300 µm and ≈ +1.3° for 500 µm, i.e. ~0.24–0.27 d/µA against 0.21. The Ø20 aperture is cheap
  only in thin low-Z rings (A3), not in the 2 mm Cu rings of §17 (9.6°).
- A broken window vents the accelerator line: keep the fast valve and the vendor burst rating in the asks.

### B3. An Al tube end next to the beam (design caution)

§17 recommends "≤ 0.5 mm Al or CFRP" for the last 40 mm of tube, and the page repeats it; §4 of the same file excludes Al
"where the beam is" because of the 992 keV ²⁷Al(p,γ) resonance (10.8 MeV γ, 0.36 % legs per γ; it was the largest
single-arm trigger source of the old baseline). With a 45° window the beam ellipse and its halo end ~10 mm from the tube
wall. A 1 % halo on 0.5 mm of Al at 1.1 MeV gives ~85 γ/s of 10.8 MeV at 1 µA (thick-target yield 1.4×10⁻⁹/p), i.e.
~0.3 legs/arm/s, small against 15/s of cosmics; a 10 % halo gives 3/s. Not a background in the window (E_sum ≤ 10.8 MeV),
but avoidable: the **CFRP tube end is better in the trace anyway (6.8°, 0.21 d)**, so prefer it, or line an Al end with
Mo/Ta, and collimate upstream in Ta.

### B4. Width of the 1030 keV resonance: text and code disagree by 6 % (cosmetic)

cm, so nothing numerical changes; write "Γ_cm 138 keV (≈ 158 keV lab; Table 8.10: 168)" in the text.

### B6. Checked and found right

Recomputed independently, all agree with the study to the quoted precision:
- **Kinematics:** E_cm = (7/8)·E_p, E* = 18.157 MeV at 1030 keV; θ_min = 2 asin(m/E_X) = 133.9° (m 16.7); the ⁸Be recoil
  β = 0.006 is negligible; the arms' polar range 48.6–131.4° (±180 mm at 204 mm).
- **Resonances:** σ_peak(441) = 4πλ̄²ω Γ_γ/Γ = 5.9 mb; the 1030 keV resonant peak 56 µb (γ₀+γ₁), so the resonance is
  ~half of the measured 93 µb and ~half of γ₀ (17 of 33 µb): the "half direct E1" split is right; γ₁/γ₀ ≈ 2 at 1 MeV.
- **IPC and the claim:** Born M1 α = 3.47×10⁻³ against Rose 3.9×10⁻³; R(ATOMKI) = 5.8×10⁻⁶ (PRL 116 042501), so
  X17/IPC = 1.5×10⁻³; the 23 % E1 share of the 2016 fit; ATOMKI 2016 ran **1.0 µA on 300 µg/cm² Li₂O on 10 µm Al**
  (checked against the PRL text in `refs/`, lines 62–63; the 700 µg/cm² in `ESTIMATES.md` is the MEG II-2026
  normalisation case, not 2016).
- **Rates:** 1 µA = 6.24×10¹² p/s; 1.85×10³ γ₀/s, ~7 IPC/s, 0.011 X17/s, ~920/day at the ATOMKI point; the 20 µm Li
  ×8.2 (ΔE 228 keV vs 61 keV, ×2.2 Li per keV); 14 µm at 45° = 20/√2.
- **Materials and vacuum:** peak fluence 2.1×10¹⁸ H/cm²/day/µA at σ 2 mm; Li–water 1.35 µm/day at 10⁻⁷ mbar (2 Li per
  H₂O, an upper bound; 1 Li per H₂O for LiOH gives 0.7); orifice conductance ~140 l/s for Ø35 mm (H₂O); p(H₂O) ~10⁻⁹
  mbar from 40 cm² of warm tube; the Cu dome pR/2t = 4.4 MPa; the dome as a radial fin ~30 K at 4.4 W; the holder arm
  4.8 K/W.
- **DAQ:** live = 1/(1 + fτ) with τ = 298 µs gives 98.3 % at 58 legs/s; 0.12–0.28 TB/day per FEU at P = 1.
- **Thresholds:** ⁷Li(p,n) 1.881 MeV, ¹⁹F(p,n) 4.2, ¹³C(p,n) 3.24, ⁶³Cu(p,n) 4.15: no neutrons at ≤ 1.3 MeV.
- **Logic of §17:** the tangent-path argument for the concave dome (recomputed: 15–18 % X₀ at 90°); the LN₂ trap
  upstream rather than a shroud; the "thin last 40 mm" geometry (r ≥ 1.13·|y| for the accepted cone); the Al-window
  answer in §9; the 3D-fit, impostor and pseudo-data chain of FEASIBILITY_SIM §14–17 (internally consistent;
  headline 1.0 d / 17 d reproduced across README, deck numbers and `reach_geant.csv` columns).
